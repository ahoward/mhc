01 the idea

An engineering company. Not a startup.

A small unit of systems engineers and scientists — each successful on their own — who embed with you and do the work themselves. Not a shop you hand work to, not a roster of names you'll never meet, and not startup theater — real software, quietly built.

Thirty years each architecting truly novel systems from a computer-science perspective — high availability, code that generates code, systems that hold when they're loaded. Production, at scale, where it matters. Not decks, not demos. That is the muscle.

You don't outsource and wait. You pair with the engineers and learn to run it yourself — working alongside private, bleeding-edge systems, not off-the-shelf tools with buttons and screens. The kind of person who'd build a cabin in Alaska or run a hundred miles: you want to understand every piece.

02 what we do

One unit. The whole build.

One team covers the full arc — so you're not stitching five vendors together, and no context leaks in the seams between them:

architecture build security & compliance data & integration delivery & ops design brand & copy positioning

Half a person of engineering and half a person of positioning beats two people of either. You book the mix the sprint actually needs — and every seat is a principal, not a hand-off.

03 the thesis

You need scientists, not programmers.

AI-enabled programming is meta-programming: precisely specifying things that specify other things — and specifying future things, which is what driving agents actually is. It demands an accurate world model and a scientific rigor most developers cannot navigate.

If you like a well-spelled-out ticket with clear business goals handed down by a stakeholder, you are irrelevant in this economy. The job now is to understand the understanding — to model the lexicon, the concepts, and the workflows of the business, and encode a worldview precise enough that a machine can both generate the system and prove it correct.

And correctness is objective and subjective. Does it make money? Is it legal? Is it corporate enough? Does it harm the people who use it? Because of how these models work, those words matter at every layer of the stack — the value you encode at each step dramatically alters the output. That is what engineering is now.

Which is why a game of telephone is fatal. Every hop between the intent and the machine — a nearshore team, an offshore team, a layer of account managers — degrades the one thing that matters, at the exact moment it matters most. So the scientists do it themselves: the person who understands your system is the person at the keyboard, talking to the models.

Humans declare intent. Machines generate implementations. Validators enforce contracts.

04 how we work

Quietly lethal.

Small, senior, moving at AI speeds — real software shipped in days and weeks, where a delegated shop would still be scoping. Daily, hands-on: two-hour sessions, several days a week. We work the way we'll teach you.

Because words are the work now — say stateless or stateful and you get a different system. That precision is exactly what a near-shored, obfuscated shop can't hold. And yes, we run dark factories — we pioneered them: three-plus years of RLHF, custom-tuned small models for specific tasks.

The old VC clock — three-to-five-year vesting? LOL. Nobody knows what tools we'll be using in three months. Alpine style: no expedition of fifteen hauling a hundred pounds up the mountain. Trail runners, a shell, a clear line to the summit. Speedboat, not Titanic — small, fast, and dangerous.

How we relate to the world: 3×5 cards, whiteboards, schematics, a sticky note with the one number that matters. Pocket-protector precision, lightweight and high-bandwidth — no ceremony, no theater.

07 who it's for

Systems that can't fail.

Real software for real operations — car makers, .gov and .mil, school systems, title monitoring, energy and mining, logistics. Regulated, load-bearing, consequential. Not the startup hype cycle — more marketing than meat — but the software an organization actually runs on.

A trucking company with 700 drivers that could build software, has never tried, and will lose if it doesn't. A cleared program that needs the work done inside the fence, by the people who understand it. We're the AI-tech partner for the things that matter — find the cutting-edge custom solution, build it, prove it, and — if they want — help them run a mini-SaaS off it. Like open source, but novel.
